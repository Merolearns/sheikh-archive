## Khutba, Fri Nov 13, 5:17 AM (full text, as sent)

فضل صلة الأرحام / The Virtue of Maintaining Family Ties / First Khutba. All praise is due to Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. We praise Him, seek His help and forgiveness, and seek refuge in Him from the evil within ourselves and from our misdeeds. I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger. May Allah send peace and blessings upon him, his family, and his companions. Servants of Allah, I urge you and myself to be mindful of Allah. He calls on believers to be mindful of Him as He deserves and to remain in submission to Him until death (Qur'an 3:102). He also reminds us that He created humanity from a single soul, commands us to honor the ties of kinship, and tells us that He is always watching over us (Qur'an 4:1). Brothers and sisters, when was the last time we called a relative simply to ask how they were doing? Do we know who in our family is sick, lonely, or struggling to pay a debt? Maintaining family ties, or silat al-rahim, is an act of worship. Allah commands justice, excellence, and generosity toward relatives, and He tells us to give relatives their due (Qur'an 16:90 and 30:38). Begin with your parents, then give attention to your closest relatives. Visit when you can, speak kindly, offer support in hardship, make du'a for them, and help reconcile those who are at odds. What you can offer will depend on your means and what your relatives need. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, 'Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should maintain their family ties.' (Al-Bukhari) The Reward of Keeping Family Ties. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught that whoever wishes for their provision to be expanded and their lifespan extended should maintain their family ties (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). These blessings come according to Allah's decree. Allah can bless modest means so that they suffice, and He can fill a person's years with worship and service. Even a small gesture can reassure a parent or bring relief to a household in need. A man once asked the Prophet what deed would lead him to Paradise. The Prophet told him to worship Allah alone, establish prayer, give zakat, and maintain family ties (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). He also gave a grave warning about severing kinship ties (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). The Qur'an likewise warns those who spread corruption and cut off their relatives (Qur'an 47:22–23). Take this as a call to repent and repair what you can while there is still time. The Prophet taught that truly maintaining family ties means reaching out even when relatives have cut you off (Al-Bukhari). If a disagreement has kept you from speaking to your brother or uncle, you might begin by offering salam or asking how they are. When Khadijah comforted the Prophet after the first revelation, she recalled how faithfully he kept his family ties and helped people in need. When Abu Talhah wished to give away his beloved garden, the Prophet encouraged him to give it to his relatives. When disputes arise over money, inheritance, or hurtful words, be fair even when you are angry. Guard your speech, respect each person's rights, and seek reconciliation where possible. Part of helping a relative who is acting unjustly is stopping them from doing harm. I say these words and ask Allah Almighty to forgive me, you, and all Muslims. Ask Him for forgiveness. He is the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful. Second Khutba. All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds. We thank Him for His blessings. I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah alone and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger. May Allah send peace and blessings upon him, his family, and his companions. Servants of Allah, begin with your parents. Call them, visit if you can, ask about their health, and pray for them often. If you live far away, set aside a regular time to call. A few minutes of conversation can ease an older relative's loneliness. Check on siblings, grandparents, aunts, and uncles. If someone is sick, out of work, or in debt, offer what you can and protect their dignity. An errand or a kind word may be what they need that day. When a disagreement is hard to resolve, ask someone wise and fair to help. If you have wronged a relative, restore what is owed and ask for forgiveness. Where there is serious harm, seek protection and advice, and keep whatever contact is safe and appropriate. Show kindness across differences of faith as well. When Asma' bint Abi Bakr asked about keeping ties with her mother, who did not share her faith, the Prophet told her to do so (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). Distance and busy lives make it easy to lose touch. Use the means you have to stay close, and give your relatives your attention when you speak with them. If you can help someone financially, do so discreetly. You can also make du'a for them, speak on their behalf when appropriate, or help them take care of something they cannot manage alone. Putting Family Ties into Practice. Today, call one relative you have not spoken to in months. Set aside time each week to check on family and set aside time each month for a visit when you can. Give a share of your charity to relatives in need. Teach your children who their relatives are and take them along on visits so they can learn what it means to care for family. Brothers and sisters, think of someone you have not heard from in a long time. Is there a relative who needs food, medicine, or help paying a debt? Is there someone you could visit while they are ill, or a family dispute you could help resolve? Life is short. The chance to hear a loved one's voice or sit with them may pass before we realize it. Let today be a new beginning in honoring our parents and caring for our relatives. Reach out to someone you have missed, help someone in need, and seek Allah's pleasure through the ties He has commanded us to keep. O Allah, help us maintain our family ties, honor our parents, and treat our relatives with kindness. Reconcile us and bring our hearts together. Bless our provision, our lives, and our deeds. Heal our sick, have mercy on those who have died, and guide us to what pleases You. May Allah send peace and blessings upon our Prophet Muhammad, his family, and his companions. Ameen.
